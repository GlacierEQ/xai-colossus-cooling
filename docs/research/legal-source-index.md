# Legal Source Index

Last updated: 2026-06-27 HST.

This file contains a preliminary source list for private research. Verify official sources before formal use. This is a source index and triage map, not legal advice and not a filing-ready memorandum.

## Operating boundary

Keep each matter separated by person, event, jurisdiction, and source. Do not merge unrelated legal matters into this research index. Every formal-use claim needs a source, evidence ID, confidence label, and review date.

## Nevada: personal injury / premises / casino-hotel triage

- **NRS 11.190** — limitations periods. The readable 2025 source states that an action for damages for injuries to a person caused by wrongful act or neglect is generally within 2 years. Use as limitations-risk check only until official statute text is verified.
  - Readable source: https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-11/statute-11-190/
  - Official source to verify: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-011.html#NRS011Sec190
- **NRS 41.141** — comparative negligence. The readable 2025 source states that comparative negligence does not bar recovery if the plaintiff's negligence was not greater than the defendant's negligence, with several-liability rules for multiple defendants.
  - Readable source: https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-41/statute-41-141/
  - Official source to verify: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-041.html#NRS041Sec141

### Premises-liability evidence checklist

- Exact property name and legal operator.
- Incident date or date range.
- Reservation/folio/payment record.
- Incident/security report.
- Medical records and photographs.
- Witness names.
- Text messages or calls close in time.
- Any proof that the hotel/casino controlled the water, room, shower, hallway, lobby, or security response.

## Nevada: divorce / identity / military-benefit issue map

- **NRS 125.010** — causes for divorce: insanity for 2 years, separate and apart for 1 year without cohabitation, or incompatibility.
  - Readable source: https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-125/statute-125-010/
  - Official source to verify: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-125.html#NRS125Sec010
- **NRS 125.020** — verified complaint, residence/domicile, and district-court jurisdiction; readable 2025 source includes the 6-week Nevada residence requirement before suit unless the cause accrued in the county while actually domiciled there.
  - Readable source: https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-125/statute-125-020/
  - Official source to verify: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-125.html#NRS125Sec020
- **NRS 125.150** — alimony, property rights, and explanation of pension or retirement-benefit provisions; readable 2025 source includes equal disposition of community property to the extent practicable and written reasons for unequal disposition.
  - Readable source: https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-125/statute-125-150/
  - Official source to verify: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-125.html#NRS125Sec150

### Divorce / identity evidence checklist

- Divorce decree or proof no decree exists.
- Spouse identity, aliases, and service status.
- Property/debt schedule.
- Pension/retirement/military-benefit language.
- Residency records for Nevada jurisdiction.

## Nevada: housing / occupancy / essential services

- **NRS 118A.290** — landlord habitability duties; readable 2025 source includes hot and cold running water, plumbing, electrical, heating, sanitation, and maintained facilities/appliances.
  - Readable source: https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-118a/statute-118a-290/
  - Official source to verify: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-118A.html#NRS118ASec290
- **NRS 118A.355** — failure to maintain habitable condition; readable 2025 source includes written notice, 14-day remedy period for remediable conditions, actual damages, termination, court relief, and rent-withholding conditions.
  - Readable source: https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-118a/statute-118a-355/
  - Official source to verify: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-118A.html#NRS118ASec355
- **NRS 118A.380** — failure to supply essential items/services; readable 2025 source includes heat, air-conditioning, running water, hot water, electricity, gas, functioning door lock, or another essential item/service, with 48-hour notice/remedy framing.
  - Readable source: https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-118a/statute-118a-380/
  - Official source to verify: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-118A.html#NRS118ASec380

### Housing evidence checklist

- Lease, receipts, messages, and notices.
- Address and dates of occupancy.
- Condition photographs/videos.
- Utility interruption records.
- Inspection/code-enforcement records.
- Rent ledger and payment proof.
- Eviction or collection notices.

## Federal: financial-account error / unauthorized-transfer research

- **15 U.S.C. 1693f** — EFTA error resolution. The readable source states that a financial institution receiving proper notice within 60 days must investigate, report results within 10 business days, may provisionally recredit within 10 business days, and generally conclude the investigation within 45 days if provisional credit is used.
  - Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1693f
- **15 U.S.C. 1693g** — consumer liability for unauthorized electronic fund transfers. The readable source includes liability limits, 60-day statement notice issues, two-business-day lost/stolen access-device issues, and burden of proof on the financial institution.
  - Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1693g

### Financial-account evidence checklist

- Full monthly statements.
- Transaction export with dates, amounts, merchants, transfer rails, and account IDs.
- Dispute notices and dates sent.
- Bank investigation letters.
- Provisional-credit records.
- Police/identity-theft reports, if any.
- Copies of documents the bank relied on.

## Federal: medical records and insurance eligibility

- **HIPAA right of access / 45 CFR 164.524** — HHS guidance says individuals generally have a legal, enforceable right to inspect or receive copies of PHI in designated record sets, including medical and billing records, and covered entities generally must provide access no later than 30 calendar days from receiving the request, with one limited 30-day extension if properly noticed.
  - Source: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/access/index.html
- **TRICARE / DEERS** — TRICARE states that registration in DEERS is required to get TRICARE benefits, sponsors must register eligible family members, and DEERS errors can cause problems with claims, billing, authorizations, and prescriptions.
  - Source: https://www.tricare.mil/Plans/Eligibility/DEERS

### Medical billing / insurance evidence checklist

- Itemized bill.
- Explanation of benefits or denial.
- Insurance eligibility proof.
- DEERS eligibility record or sponsor documentation.
- HIPAA records request and delivery log.
- Hospital financial-assistance application.
- Call log with date, time, person, and reference number.

## New York: hospital billing / financial assistance / surprise bills

- **NY Public Health Law 2807-k** — New York Senate source shows general hospitals must establish financial-aid policies/procedures for low-income uninsured, underinsured, or exhausted-benefit patients; notices must inform patients that financial aid may be available; completed applications can pause bills until decision; payment plans have limits; and hospitals must make reasonable efforts before collection activity.
  - Source: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PBH/2807-K
- **NY DFS surprise medical bills** — DFS source states that surprise-bill protections can limit patient responsibility to in-network cost-sharing in qualifying circumstances; uninsured/self-pay patients may have Good Faith Estimate and independent dispute rights.
  - Source: https://www.dfs.ny.gov/consumers/health_insurance/surprise_medical_bills

### Vehicle / records evidence checklist

- Registration and title records.
- VIN and plate photographs.
- Citation packets.
- Tow records and release records.
- Police report numbers and incident history.
- Any statement that another vehicle used the plates.
- Communications with tow yard, DMV, police records, and insurer.
